## 1. The domain (what this kind of app is called, and the human need)

**Summary:** The dominant industry term is **"personal CRM"** (also "relationship management app," newer framing "relationship intelligence platform"). The underlying human feeling — wanting to stay connected but letting time slip — is real and well-documented: people underestimate how much a reach-out is appreciated, and relationships decay through neglect, not conflict. Busy people need low-friction structure, not recall. The space is dominated by professional/networking tools; the strongest consumer-oriented, non-CRM-feeling entrants are **Monica, Mesh (formerly Clay), and Tapestry** — but even these lean on CRM-style contact management or frequency tracking, leaving room for a consumer app that never feels like a CRM.

Key sourced claims:
- The category self-describes as "personal CRM" — Dex ("Personal CRM for your relationships"), Streak's roundup ("personal CRM tools and relationship management apps"). Sources: getdex.com; streak.com/post/personal-crm-tools-apps
- Anti-CRM reframings exist: Tapestry — "Not another contacts app. A relationship intelligence platform... a fitness tracker for your relationships" (tapestrycrm.com); Monica — "A CRM, but nobody is a lead" (monicahq.com)
- Most existing tools are professional/networking oriented (Dex → entrepreneurs/investors; Covve → business-card scanning; LeadDelta → LinkedIn pipeline) — consumer-facing non-CRM apps are underserved.
- **The psychology:** people "significantly underestimate how much others will appreciate being reached out to" (Liu, U. Pittsburgh) — the more unexpected the communication, the bigger the gap. Source: nicenews.com/culture/reach-out-friends-study/; theguardian.com
- **"Relationship drift"** is an established term: neglect-driven decay via proximity bias, cognitive housekeeping, threshold effect, interactional inertia. Source: blog.extndly.com/why-we-lose-touch-the-psychology-of-relationship-drift/
- Close bonds need 200+ hours of invested time (Hall, U. Kansas). Source: news.ku.edu
- Dunbar's number: ~150 stable relationships; an inner core of ~5 gets ~40% of social time, ~15 people get two-thirds. Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar%27s_number
- Loneliness is a documented public-health issue (2023 Surgeon General advisory: ~half of U.S. adults experience loneliness). Source: hhs.gov
- Harvard Study of Adult Development (85+ years): relationships are the primary driver of lifelong well-being. *(Truncated in the notification; full finding in the journal.)*

## 2. Current best practice (2026)

**Summary:** Treat privacy and data ownership as the core value prop (the local-first movement is the canonical framing; tooling has matured — OPFS, RxDB, Automerge, Yjs, Electric). Minimize relationship administration (reference apps win by auto-capturing context and gentle prompts, not demanding data entry). Keep onboarding minimal; defer permission/notification requests until value is understood in-context. Notification discipline (request on a user gesture; timely/relevant/precise nudges). Delivery intents for private individual messages: **Web Share API** (absent in desktop Firefox), **PWA Share Target** (needs install, not Baseline), **sms:** (IETF RFC 5724), WhatsApp wa.me links.

Key things a stale guide would get wrong (all 2023-era changes):
- Web Push arrived on iOS only in iOS 16.4 (2023), only for installed home-screen PWAs.
- Push API and OPFS became Baseline in March 2023.
- Android 13 (2022) made notifications a runtime permission.
- CRDT libraries are mature (Rust/Swift bindings).
- AI tone-drafting is now cheap and mature (1M-token context, streaming, Zero-Data-Retention / data-residency controls) — fits a privacy-sensitive app.

## 3. Common pitfalls (what breaks first)

**Summary — the sharpest edges:**
1. **Local-first data can vanish.** iOS Safari evicts IndexedDB/Cache/localStorage after 7 days of non-use (installed home-screen PWAs exempt); browsers treat storage as best-effort and may wipe it. localStorage is the wrong store (5MB, synchronous, strings-only, no SW access). → Ship persistence requests, install encouragement, sync/conflict handling, and **export/backup**, or users lose years of memories.
2. **Mobile-layout traps that break first:** ignoring `env(safe-area-inset-*)` hides UI under the notch/home indicator; inputs under 16px trigger iOS auto-zoom; `100vh` hides content behind dynamic toolbars (use `svh`/`dvh`); undersized tap targets (44pt Apple); iOS honors only the standalone display mode with `apple-touch-icon` or the icon becomes a screenshot.
3. **Notifications:** permission is a one-shot asset — prompting cold makes users block and the browser never re-asks. Use a double-permission flow. Realistic web-push opt-in is ~10–15%, settling to ~5–8%. On iOS, push only exists for installed standalone PWAs after a direct user gesture.
4. **AI tone-drafting on intimate messages carries an "AI disclosure penalty"** — recipients judge senders lazy/insincere once they learn a message was AI-written; 53% of Americans say AI hurts privacy. → Design it as an **editable draft in the user's own voice, never autosend**, keep private data on-device where possible.
5. **PWA deploy:** users silently stay on the old version because a new service worker installs then "waits" until all old tabs close (use `skipWaiting`/`clientsClaim` + update UI); never rename the SW file; a SW caches nothing by itself — version caches and purge old ones.

## 5. Messaging feasibility + compliance review (early, before the architecture is locked)

**VERDICT (recommended approach):** **Per-recipient native composer launching** — iOS `MFMessageComposeViewController`; Android `ACTION_SENDTO` with `sms:` scheme — **one composer per recipient, never a multi-recipient array.**

Why this approach:
- Keeps each message a genuine 1:1 SMS sent from the user's own number — recipients can never see each other (the privacy core).
- **Avoids the entire A2P 10DLC registration/consent/fee/filtering regime**, because the user's own device sends each message and the user taps Send.
- Clears App Store review via Guideline 5.1.1(v) as long as you omit "Select All," force individualized per-recipient selection, and preview the message and sender.

Hard tradeoffs (unavoidable with native sending):
- ~N taps for N recipients (12 taps for 12 people).
- No in-app reply surfacing — replies land in the phone's Messages app, not inside Circle Nurture.
- No delivery/read analytics.

If in-app reply surfacing and delivery tracking become hard requirements, the only path is a provider API — but that changes the sender identity to a business number (dilutes the "private conversation from me" feel) and imposes the full A2P/TCPA compliance burden on the user as sender.

**Two hard NOs:** do NOT use a multi-recipient `recipients[]` array (creates a group MMS — violates the privacy core); do NOT pursue programmatic sending via SMS permissions on Google Play (only a default SMS handler can hold SEND_SMS; it will not be approved).
